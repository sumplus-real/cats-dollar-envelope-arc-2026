CATS Dollar Envelope helps an agent user see the full dollar budget and keep an independent way out if the service disappears. A contract caps task principal and acceptance fees; a versioned local envelope separately budgets network fees for the owner and both operators, including pending/Unknown reservations and owner exit fees. A downloadable Recovery Kit checks the live chain and lets the fixed owner recover only unaccepted funds. A dollar receipt independently reconciles the principal, all signer transaction fees and balance changes.

Arc makes this useful in one unit: native USDC for principal, payments and Gas. We use 18-decimal native amounts and verify deterministic receipts, matching Accepted events and same-block state. The 6-decimal ERC-20 view is the same balance, not a second asset.

The fixed demonstration starts with 0.010 USDC. A accepts 0.002; B0 accepts 0.003; B1 remains Offered at 0.004. After the main service stops, the owner uses the independent kit to withdraw the unaccepted 0.005. B's 0.003 accepted credit remains owed and can be claimed independently. Accepted fees are irreversible and do not prove service delivery or quality. App Gas limits do not constrain unrelated wallet activity. Chain, wallet, RPC, Gas and chain-level transfer rules remain dependencies.

This is an unaudited research prototype by Jakob Xu / Sumplus, continuing the existing MIT CATS SpendPass budget tree with Arc-native accounting, signer fee envelopes, portable recovery and independent receipts. Operator responses are fixtures, with no paid model, TEE or production-performance claim. AI tools assisted development. This work has received no Circle/Arc funding.
